THE MAKEUP STORE HRM — IMPROVEMENT REVIEW
24 November 2026 · Local source assessment · No product changes or production writes

Your shift concern is correct: an employee’s ordinary change request should be blocked once attendance begins for that shift, and the lock should remain after clock-out. Future unworked shifts should still be eligible. A manager/admin correction should be an explicit audited exception.

The highest-value improvements are consistent business rules, dependable retries, accurate time/pay calculations, and simpler completion/approval workflows. The current app has useful foundations: attendance identity checks, server-generated attendance event times, an admin-only helper for privileged corrections, cancellable GPS refinement, and visible loading/retry states. Several rules currently stop at the screen rather than being enforced end to end.

This review provides 12 improvement groups and a technical appendix of 35 findings. Priorities are proposed implementation order, not promises about effort or claims that every possible defect has been found. No fixes have been applied; existing uncommitted product changes were preserved.

PRIORITIZED IMPROVEMENTS

1. [First] Lock a shift once attendance starts
Keep the shift assignment, date and scheduled hours fixed after clock-in, including after clock-out. Check both sides of an interchange or peer swap. Apply the rule to requests, approval and bulk scheduling. Future unrelated shifts should remain editable.
Expected experience: An employee who has started today’s 10:00 shift cannot move it to 12:00 or exchange that worked day. A manager sees why it is locked. A historical correction uses a separate audited workflow.
Technical references: F01, F02, F03, F04, F05

2. [First] Make clock-in and clock-out safe to retry
Use a unique operation ID and an exact attendance session for each swipe. Enforce one active session in the database, reject failed eligibility reads, and confirm saved state after an uncertain network response. Do not wait for push delivery before acknowledging attendance.
Expected experience: If a phone loses its connection after a swipe, reopening or retrying shows the saved result instead of creating another attendance row or closing a newer session.
Technical references: F06, F07, F09, F10, F32

3. [First] Improve location feedback and verification
Keep the existing quick-location/refinement flow, but validate finite coordinates and store configuration on the server too. Give clear permission, accuracy and timeout messages. Add a bounded attendance-save timeout with reconciliation. Provide a reasoned manager-assisted exception when legitimate staff cannot obtain GPS.
Expected experience: Staff see whether the app is finding a location or saving attendance. Missing store coordinates cannot silently produce a verified clock-in. Clock-out remains available without GPS.
Technical references: F08, F09, F32

4. [First] Make re-entry and corrections explicit
Keep original attendance events, link each request to an exact attendance record, require a reason and single-use approval, and preserve who approved it. Distinguish correcting an accidental checkout from returning after an actual off-duty break.
Expected experience: Approving an accidental-checkout correction cannot reopen an unrelated day or be reused repeatedly. Real break time is not silently paid as continuous work.
Technical references: F11, F12

5. [First] Use one store clock and reliable time calculations
Use Asia/Kolkata for business dates and convert event times consistently. Handle overnight shifts, half-open day/month boundaries and cross-month leave. Share calculation helpers between attendance, payroll and reports.
Expected experience: Manual 10:00 entry means 10:00 IST everywhere. A shift ending after midnight belongs to the correct shift day. Reports agree regardless of the server or viewer timezone.
Technical references: F12, F13, F14, F18

6. [First] Make payroll period-based and reviewable
Calculate amounts on the server, check payroll authorization, use precise worked minutes, filter payments by month and year, and prevent duplicate settlement. Show regular hours, overtime, rate, advances, adjustments and net pay. Confirm whether overtime is a total 1.5x rate or an additional premium.
Expected experience: A prior-year November advance does not reduce this November’s pay. The current formula pays overtime hours at a combined 2.5x rate when Auto OT is added; the app should apply your chosen policy explicitly.
Technical references: F15, F16, F17, F18

7. [Next] Turn shift and leave requests into clear workflows
Store request type, target preset/date and affected shift IDs as structured data. Show a before/after preview, current status, decision reason and timeline. Prevent duplicates and reject stale approvals. Check worked shifts, overlapping leave and staffing conflicts.
Expected experience: Employees select “change time” or “move day off” without ambiguous generated messages. A request cancelled or invalidated by clock-in cannot later be approved from an old browser tab.
Technical references: F02, F03, F04, F05, F14

8. [Next] Repair checklist setup and trustworthy completion
Separate reusable template items from daily run items. Create one appropriate checklist per store/day/type, support staff collaboration, require mandatory items/photo proof before completion, and freeze completed runs unless explicitly reopened.
Expected experience: Opening and closing checklists contain the intended template items. A colleague can finish their assigned item, and an empty checklist cannot count as a completed opening.
Technical references: F23, F24

9. [Next] Make task and audit actions match employee responsibilities
Let employees complete authorized assigned work with an explicit Complete action. Keep cancelled work closed. Restrict audit execution to managers/admins, validate scores, preserve template versions, and fix audit links plus record deletion.
Expected experience: Repeating a completion request does not undo a task. A five-point audit question cannot receive 999 points. Starting an audit opens its actual detail page.
Technical references: F25, F26, F27, F28

10. [Next] Keep photos and forms reliable on phones
Fix multi-image selection, accumulate uploaded URLs correctly, compress images, validate files on the server, save drafts, and clean up cancelled uploads. Save each report and its child records atomically or with an explicit resumable state.
Expected experience: Selecting three photos keeps all three. An interrupted upload does not silently lose proof or leave duplicate empty damage/restock reports.
Technical references: F29, F30

11. [Next] Enforce role, store and privacy rules in the database
Bind authorship to the signed-in user, restrict approvals to authorized reviewers, preserve store ownership, scope manager access, and limit private report/notification reads. Reconcile migrations before applying new policies. Use a generic offline shell for private HR pages on shared devices.
Expected experience: Employee, manager and admin permissions are consistent even when someone bypasses the screen. Signing out does not leave the previous user’s private page available through offline navigation.
Technical references: F19, F20, F21, F22, F31, F33

12. [Later] Show actionable alerts and accurate operations reports
Prioritize today’s active shift, pending approvals and due tasks. Deliver notifications to the intended person after the business transaction. Add real period filters and compliance metrics, avoid penalizing cancelled/override requests as unreliability, and verify recurring tasks, expiry reminders and auto-checkout jobs. Test Android login, GPS, reconnect and report saving on a real device.
Expected experience: Managers can distinguish an overdue task from a completed one, see last successful automation runs, and reconcile dashboard numbers with actual records. Alerts are useful rather than noisy.
Technical references: F18, F31, F32, F34, F35

EXPECTED SHIFT-CHANGE RULES
• Future shift with no attendance: Allow an otherwise valid request, with conflict and eligibility checks.
• Today, before clock-in: Allow if your request cutoff policy permits it; recheck when approving.
• Clocked in / attendance active: Block ordinary changes to that shift’s employee, date and scheduled hours.
• Clocked out / auto-checked out: Keep the ordinary shift-change lock. Checkout does not erase that the shift was worked.
• Two-day interchange or peer swap: Block if either affected shift has attendance or otherwise conflicts.
• Request pending when employee clocks in: Allow attendance under the current valid schedule; invalidate/expire the incompatible request atomically or reject its later approval.
• Unrelated future shift while employee is clocked in today: Continue allowing eligible requests for that future shift.
• Accidental checkout / historical correction: Use an exact attendance ID, single-use approval, reason and preserved original events; keep it separate from ordinary shift change.

ACCEPTANCE SCENARIOS FOR THE FIRST IMPLEMENTATION
• Clock in, then attempt a time change, date move, peer swap and bulk schedule change: all ordinary mutations affecting the worked shift are rejected consistently.
• Submit a request, then clock in before approval; approving the stale request returns a clear conflict.
• Clock out and attempt the same changes: the shift remains locked. Another future shift is still requestable.
• Source is unworked but target/peer shift was worked: the exchange is blocked.
• Double swipe, two phones, delayed/replayed requests and failed response after a successful commit produce one intended attendance outcome.
• GPS permission denied, slow, inaccurate or missing store location produces clear feedback without false verification. Clock-out still works without GPS.
• Correct an accidental checkout once with reason/approver; preserve original events and reject replay. A real break remains excluded from worked time.
• Manually enter 10:00 IST and verify the identical business time in logs, reports and payroll. Verify overnight and month/year boundaries.
• Two simultaneous settlements and a network retry create one intended payment operation; a prior-year payment does not reduce this period’s balance.
• Employee direct API/database attempts cannot self-approve reports, alter another store’s work, or read another user’s private notifications.

TECHNICAL APPENDIX — DETAILED FINDINGS

F01 [P1] A worked shift is not locked against ordinary changes
Area: Shift changes
Verification: Confirmed in application source and isolated execution; live database trigger protection unverified.
Observed behavior: The request button checks only whether the selected date is today or later. Submission and ordinary approval never query attendance. Bulk publishing also upserts scheduled times without checking whether a shift has been worked. The defect persists after clock-out: no path treats any recorded check-in as an immutable boundary.
Reproduction: With active attendance for today's shift, submit a time-change request and approve it. The isolated test changes the shift from 10:00 to 12:00 while attendance remains open. Repeat with completed attendance, or submit before clock-in and approve afterward; no attendance guard exists.
Business impact: The schedule underlying lateness, overtime and accountability can change after work starts. Hiding the button would leave the API, Server Action, approval screen and bulk scheduler paths open.
Improvement: Enforce the invariant in a shared server/database transaction used by clock-in and schedule mutation. Lock all affected shift rows in consistent order and reject ordinary changes if any attendance exists. Keep this lock after checkout. Make historical corrections a separate, reasoned, audited admin operation.

F02 [P1] Requests accept foreign, past and duplicate shift references
Area: Shift changes
Verification: Confirmed handler behavior; whether arbitrary references can persist depends on live RLS and constraints.
Observed behavior: Authentication binds userId to the session, but neither entry point verifies that shiftId belongs to that user, represents an eligible date, or has no conflicting pending request. Receiver is set to the caller regardless of who owns the referenced shift.

Improvement: Derive the requestor from auth; fetch and authorize every affected shift. Validate both dates, target eligibility, approved leave and conflicts. Add a structured uniqueness rule for competing pending requests and a submission idempotency key.

F03 [P1] Approval has no pending-state or schedule-version precondition
Area: Approvals
Verification: Confirmed source and isolated execution.
Observed behavior: The approval handler trusts the request object previously loaded into the browser, then updates by ID alone. Cancellation also updates by ID alone. There is no atomic pending-to-approved transition, expected shift version, or recheck after the manager opens a request.
Reproduction: Load a request in an admin tab, cancel it elsewhere, then approve from the stale tab. The isolated handler accepts a cancelled request. Two reviewers can also act on the same stale request, or a shift can change before approval.
Business impact: Cancelled, outdated or already handled requests can take effect; the last writer determines the displayed status and schedule.
Improvement: Move approval to one authorized transactional operation with status='pending', expected versions and row locks. Competing operations should return a conflict instead of silently overwriting each other. Revalidate attendance inside that same operation.

F04 [P1] A swap can be partially applied and still marked approved
Area: Approvals
Verification: Confirmed by executing the actual approval handler with an injected second-write failure.
Observed behavior: Two-shift interchange and peer swaps issue separate updates and ignore some returned errors. Status is updated afterward. Malformed messages, absent joined shifts, or unmatched branches can also reach the final approved update without a schedule change.
Reproduction: Fail the second shift update in the isolated harness. The first shift changes, the second does not, and the request becomes approved. A malformed 'Shift Change' message is also approved with no schedule mutation.
Business impact: Duplicate shift assignments, lost reciprocal swaps and an approval history that claims a change occurred when it did not.
Improvement: Apply every schedule mutation and request status change in one database transaction. Validate an exact request type before executing it, require the expected affected row counts, and roll back on any failure.

F08 [P1] Server geofence validation fails open for invalid data or missing configuration
Area: Attendance / location
Verification: Confirmed application guard behavior. Malformed coordinates may still be rejected by production column types/constraints.
Observed behavior: Only undefined coordinates are rejected. Numeric type, finiteness and latitude/longitude ranges are unchecked. A nonnumeric value yields NaN, which does not satisfy distance > radius. If either store coordinate is null or zero, the server skips the check but still records is_within_geofence=true. GPS age and accuracy are checked only in the browser, and neither is sent to the server.
Reproduction: In the harness, an invalid string reaches attendance.insert. With missing store coordinates, a remote coordinate is accepted and marked inside the geofence. These tests prove application behavior, not successful malformed-value persistence in the live database.
Business impact: Location validation can be bypassed or produce a false assertion of verification. Even valid coordinates remain employee-supplied claims; the current design does not prove physical presence.
Improvement: Validate strict finite coordinate ranges and a positive configured radius; reject invalid store configuration. Record location timestamp/accuracy and the actual verification result. If stronger presence assurance is required, add a separate store/device verification factor. Do not describe browser GPS alone as tamper-proof.

F10 [P1] Clock-out is not tied to the attendance session the employee intended to close
Area: Attendance
Verification: Confirmed source and isolated execution.
Observed behavior: Clock-out accepts only userId and updates every open attendance row for that employee. It has no expected attendance ID, operation key or session version. A delayed old request can therefore close a newer reopened/re-entered session.

Improvement: Require attendance_id plus an operation ID/version. Update only that employee's expected active session, and return a stable already-completed result for a retry. Handle unexpected extra open rows as an explicit reconciliation issue.

F13 [P1] UTC, Indian local time and server/browser local time are mixed
Area: Dates and time
Verification: Confirmed inconsistent source calculations; exact scheduled timestamp semantics need live column/schema verification.
Observed behavior: Scheduling writes wall-clock strings without an offset, approval writes Z, manual entry appends +00 to local-looking input, while normal attendance is a real server instant. Improvement: Use Asia/Kolkata as the explicit business timezone, real UTC instants for events, and a separate shift business date. Convert wall-clock inputs once. Use half-open local-day/month intervals and explicit next-day shift ends. Reuse the same calculations across payroll, reports and attendance.

Several queries replace errors with empty arrays/zero counts, and update actions often treat error=null as success without checking that any row was changed. The notification UI marks items read even if its action failed.
Reproduction: Separate server processes have separate limiter Maps; a new key starts a new allowance. Whether a client can influence the forwarded value needs deployment verification. Deny an UPDATE with RLS or pass an unknown ID: a zero-row response can still be reported as success. Fail schedule/stat queries: some screens display no shifts/zero counts instead of an error.
Business impact: Reduced brute-force resistance, username enumeration, growing limiter memory and operational failures that look like valid empty data or successful work.
Improvement: Use a bounded shared limiter with a trusted proxy identity and normalized account/IP policy. Keep login errors generic. Require affected rows/versions for writes, propagate query failures, and show retryable errors instead of zero-valued operational reports. Preserve optimistic UI state only after success or roll it back on failure.
